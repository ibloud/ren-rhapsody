# The Ledger — Experience Hypothesis

**Build target:** Vertical Slice v2 / pre-UEFN playtest
**Status:** Testable hypothesis

## Why this exists

The Ledger v1 establishes a coherent economic model and a stable web reference implementation. External feedback indicates that technical coherence is not yet evidence that the system produces a compelling player experience. The next gate is therefore experiential validation, not additional specification for its own sake.

## Primary experience hypothesis

> **Players should feel the weight of economic tradeoffs when time, cash, and future options are constrained.**

The economic model is the mechanism that creates this experience. The target is not merely that players understand opportunity cost, nor that they can maximize a displayed number.

### Distinguish the possible outcomes

These are different hypotheses and should not be conflated:

- **Economic reasoning:** players understand and can explain the tradeoffs.
- **Empathy for gig workers:** players report or demonstrate a stronger understanding of the pressures faced by workers navigating gig income.
- **Precarity:** players experience uncertainty, constraint, and the consequences of having limited room for error.

The first playtest should test the primary experience above while collecting evidence that helps distinguish these outcomes. It should not claim to prove empathy or broader social impact from a five-player qualitative session.

## Decision-design requirement

At least one scenario must create a meaningful choice between competing offers rather than a simple comparison of gross payout.

The first richer scenario should include:

- a lower-gross, shorter, more predictable offer that preserves future options;
- a higher-gross, longer, more exposed offer that consumes more time and may eliminate a better later opportunity;
- enough information for players to reason about both without an objectively dominant displayed answer.

The intended observation is whether players deliberate about consequences or simply select the largest visible number.

## First-session hierarchy

The player should have one legible question:

> **How do I make good choices when I cannot take every opportunity?**

The ARG is secondary enrichment. It should not compete with the economic loop for primary attention during this gate.

## Test order

1. Verify first-90-second comprehension.
2. Observe whether players deliberate on consequential choices.
3. Ask what players believe they were trying to get better at.
4. Observe emotional language and behavior around constrained choices.
5. Record ARG discovery separately, without prompting.
6. Only after the core loop is interesting, redesign the ARG bridge if discovery remains weak.
